# A2: the dispatch cost that made decode peak at four threads

22 September 2026. Source base: `6ea2939` on `a5-arm-throughput`, with the
local NEON flush and kernelbench thread-count changes already in the tree, plus
the pool spin-budget change this report measures. Host, toolchain and file
hashes are in [metadata](WSL-20260922-A2-metadata.json).

The roadmap has carried "decode peaks at four threads and declines" as the open
part of A2 since the pool merged. This is the diagnosis and the fix. The
headline is that the decline was never a property of the memory system or of
Amdahl's law: it was the cost of waking parked workers, 210 times per token.

## Diagnosis

Three measurements, each isolating one candidate cause. All are reproducible
with `cargo run --release -p tritd --example decode_profile`, which wraps the
injected `MatvecBackend` in a timing decorator and therefore needs no change to
the runtime to attribute decode time.

### 1. Where decode time actually goes

`decode_profile --threads N --tokens 32 --runs 2`, four prompts, 256 decoded
tokens. The wrapper asserts 210 ternary and one dense call per token, so a
mis-attributed window fails loudly rather than reporting a plausible-looking
split.

| threads | total ms/tok | ternary | dense head | other (serial) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1  | 53.01 | 34.09 | 15.67 | 3.25 |
| 2  | 41.44 | 23.40 | 14.73 | 3.31 |
| 4  | 39.24 | 23.47 | 12.29 | 3.48 |
| 8  | 41.70 | 25.69 | 12.43 | 3.58 |
| 16 | 49.75 | 33.53 | 12.62 | 3.60 |

The entire decline lives in the ternary projections: 23.47 ms at four threads
to 33.53 at sixteen. The dense head is flat to improving. The serial scalar
remainder -- attention, RoPE, the norm/quantize stages, the integer MLP fold,
the residual adds -- is 3.3 to 3.6 ms and barely moves, so **Amdahl's law is
not the explanation**; a fixed serial fraction flattens a curve, it does not
bend it back down.

### 2. Does a wider split buy bandwidth at all?

`decode_profile --threads N --bw-probe` streams 535 MB of planes -- a token's
worth of ternary traffic -- in 4.42 MB tiles, under two partitionings of
identical work. `static` forks once for the whole sweep, so each worker owns a
fixed row band of every tile and the barrier is paid once. `pool` issues one
`CpuBackend::matvec` per tile, which is what decode does today.

| threads | static GB/s | pool GB/s |
|---|---|---|
| 1  | 14.3 | 14.6 |
| 2  | 27.0 | 27.1 |
| 4  | 41.4 | 40.8 |
| 8  | 42.8 | **26.9** |
| 16 | 43.5 | **16.9** |
| 32 | 42.3 | **16.1** |

The memory system saturates near 43 GB/s from four threads on and **stays
there**. It does not degrade at eight, sixteen or thirty-two. Only the
per-matvec dispatch does. That rules out memory-system contention and puts the
whole gap on dispatch.

### 3. What makes dispatch expensive

`decode_profile --threads N --pool-probe` runs the real pool with a closure that
does nothing, sweeping the gap left between dispatches. Microseconds per
dispatch:

| pool | slots | gap 0us | gap 10us | gap 20us | gap 50us | gap 100us |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4  | 4  | 1.94 | 4.04 | 3.69 | 50.91 | 60.69 |
| 8  | 8  | 13.85 | 6.10 | 92.84 | 112.20 | 117.75 |
| 16 | 16 | 217.74 | 218.88 | 220.65 | 229.59 | 240.98 |

Two separate mechanisms, and the first is the one that mattered:

- **Parking.** At four slots a dispatch costs 1-4 us while workers are still
  spinning and 50-60 us once they have parked. The old window was 4000 `pause`
  instructions, chosen against a belief -- recorded in `pool.rs` -- that jobs
  arrive about 300 us apart. Measurement 1 says the serial work between
  consecutive matvecs averages 17 us, and four of a layer's seven projections
  follow a longer gap. The window was expiring inside decode's own gaps, so
  most of the 210 dispatches per token paid a futex round trip.
- **Slot-count contention.** Even with zero gap, sixteen slots cost 218 us per
  dispatch. That is the shared task mutex, the shared completion counter and a
  serial loop of sixteen `unpark` calls. It is a real second defect and is
  **not** fixed here; see Limitations.

## The change

`crates/trit-cpu/src/pool.rs`: a waiting worker now spins against a **deadline**
rather than an iteration count, defaulting to 200 us and overridable with
`TRIT_POOL_SPIN_US`. An iteration count also means a different duration on every
core, where a deadline does not. The budget restarts after each job, so it
measures idleness since the last dispatch and an idle model still parks
promptly. Nothing about the barrier, the memory model or the work partitioning
changed.

The budget value is not delicate. Interleaved at four threads, 200 / 500 /
1000 / 2000 us gave medians of 33.17 / 34.01 / 32.74 / 35.22 tok/s -- inside
this host's drift. 200 us was kept because it captures the gain while still
parking promptly, which matters for the four-core edge parts this runtime
targets.

## Result

Interleaved A/B, alternating binaries within one session so host drift hits
both arms, one process per thread count, `tritd bench` over the four short
prompts, 32 tokens, median of three (six at one thread).

| threads | before tok/s | after tok/s | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1  | 17.996 | 17.947 | 1.00x |
| 2  | 26.018 | 28.618 | 1.10x |
| 4  | 26.467 | 33.417 | 1.26x |
| 8  | 24.341 | 34.790 | **1.43x** |
| 16 | 20.295 | 33.337 | **1.64x** |

One thread is unchanged, as it must be: that path never constructs a pool.

**The four-to-eight decline is gone; the curve still turns over after eight.**
It now rises to its peak at eight threads instead of having already peaked at
four, and one-to-eight scaling goes from 1.35x to 1.94x. Sixteen threads
remains below eight -- 33.337 against 34.790 -- so this moved the peak rather
than removing every decline. What is left of that is the slot-count contention
in the Limitations section, and measurement 2 says closing it would not raise
the peak.

Canonical run through `benches/run.sh`, one process per thread count,
[raw CSV](WSL-20260922-A2.csv):

| threads | tok/s | TTFT min (ms) | achieved GB/s | probe GB/s | fraction | RSS MiB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1  | 17.017 | 391.0 | 20.0 | 44.6 | 44.9% | 1221 |
| 2  | 26.335 | 228.8 | 31.0 | 48.0 | 64.6% | 1221 |
| 4  | 33.020 | 188.0 | 38.9 | 52.1 | 74.7% | 1221 |
| 8  | **34.610** | 187.7 | 40.8 | 51.2 | **79.6%** | 1220 |
| 16 | 32.893 | 198.6 | 38.7 | 50.9 | 76.1% | 1219 |

The post-fix phase split, same profiler as measurement 1:

| threads | total ms/tok | ternary | dense head | other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4  | 28.56 | 12.68 (41.1 GB/s) | 12.54 (52.4 GB/s) | 3.34 |
| 8  | 29.65 | 13.09 (39.8 GB/s) | 12.84 (51.1 GB/s) | 3.72 |
| 16 | 29.95 | 13.31 (39.1 GB/s) | 12.65 (51.9 GB/s) | 3.98 |

The ternary path now runs at 39-47 GB/s per tensor against the 41-43 GB/s the
`static` probe says this access pattern can reach, so the gap that measurement
2 exposed is closed. Ternary and the dense head are now within 2% of each other
in time, which is the balance the byte split (44% / 56%) always predicted and
which the old dispatch cost was hiding.

## What it costs

Spinning is not free and the trade is asymmetric by thread count. Identical
workload (`tritd run --steps 128`), median of three, CPU seconds are user+sys
for the whole process:

| threads | wall before | wall after | CPU/token before | CPU/token after | sys before | sys after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1  | 8.54 | 8.04 | 66.6 ms | 63.5 ms | 0.22 | 0.20 |
| 2  | 5.85 | 5.45 | 84.7 ms | 85.1 ms | 0.69 | 0.22 |
| 4  | 5.66 | 4.50 | 136.3 ms | 132.3 ms | 1.48 | 0.38 |
| 8  | 6.02 | 4.50 | 222.1 ms | 253.5 ms | 2.79 | 0.47 |
| 16 | 7.29 | 4.73 | 373.4 ms | 532.6 ms | 4.99 | 0.77 |

**At one, two and four threads this is a strict improvement: the same or less
CPU per token, and less wall time.** The futex traffic it removes costs more
than the spinning it adds -- system time falls by 4-6x at every width. Above
four threads it buys latency with CPU: +14% CPU per token at eight and +43% at
sixteen.

That trade is stated rather than resolved, because it cannot be resolved here.
Whether spinning costs or saves **energy** per token is not measured and is not
inferable from CPU time: a spinning core keeps a package out of its idle states,
and finishing a token sooner shortens the window. No RAPL zone is exposed in
this environment, so J/token stays absent. A4 remains the milestone that answers
it.

## Limitations

- **x86-64 only, WSL2.** No ARM timing was taken and none is claimed. The
  change is portable Rust with no ISA-specific code, but a `park`/`unpark`
  round trip costs something different on every scheduler, so the size of this
  effect on other hosts is unknown until measured there.
- **No energy measurement.** See above.
- **No baseline comparison in this run.** The CSV here records those rows as
  unavailable because the harness environment variables were unset, not because
  the baselines were missing: both `llama-bench` builds and both GGUFs were
  present the whole time. They were re-run the next day, and the three-way
  result is in [WSL-20260923-compare.md](WSL-20260923-compare.md). The figures
  in this report are Tritium-only and stand as measured.
- **Slot-count contention is unfixed.** 218 us per empty dispatch at sixteen
  slots, with workers hot, is the shared `Mutex<Option<Task>>` that every worker
  locks, the single `done` counter every worker increments, and a serial
  `unpark` loop. It is why sixteen threads still trails eight. Fixing it would
  not raise the peak -- measurement 2 says the memory system has nothing left to
  give above four threads -- so it was left alone rather than traded against the
  risk of reworking the barrier.
- **k and v projections never parallelise, and that is now visible.** At
  640x2560 they are 0.41 MB, below twice `BYTES_PER_SLOT`, so `slots_for`
  returns 1 and they run serial at 12-14 GB/s while every other tensor reaches
  42-47. They cost 1.76-2.00 ms/token, about 14% of ternary time. Lowering
  `TRIT_BYTES_PER_SLOT` to 128 KiB splits them three ways and takes that row
  from 1.81 to 0.78 ms/token at four threads -- a real, repeatable phase-level
  improvement. It did **not** survive end-to-end: interleaved at n=4, four
  threads gave 35.51 vs 35.28 and eight gave 35.22 vs 36.31, both inside host
  drift. The default is therefore unchanged and this is recorded as a measured
  opportunity, not a shipped win.
- **The host drifts.** Single runs on this machine vary by up to 10%. Every
  comparison above is interleaved for that reason, and single-sample figures in
  the diagnosis section are labelled as diagnostics.
- **One thread at n=3 looked like a 3% regression** with non-overlapping ranges;
  at n=6 it is a tie. Small-sample non-overlap on this host is not evidence.
